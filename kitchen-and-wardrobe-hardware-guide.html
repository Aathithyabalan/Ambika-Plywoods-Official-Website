Kitchen and Wardrobe Hardware Guide

14 Oct 2026 5 min read By Ambika Plywoods, Dindigul

Most people spend their budget on the board and the finish, then fit the cheapest hardware. That is backwards. Hinges and drawer channels are the parts you use hundreds of times a year, and they are the first things to fail. Here is how to choose well.

Hinges

  • Choose soft-close hinges for kitchen and wardrobe shutters. They protect the shutters and cut noise.
  • Pick the right type: full overlay for shutters that cover the carcass, half overlay where two shutters share a side panel, and inset for flush doors.
  • Use enough hinges: two for small shutters, three or more for tall or heavy ones.
  • Look for a stainless or well-plated finish that resists rust in humid weather.

Drawer channels

  • Telescopic channels are basic and affordable and fine for light drawers.
  • Soft-close ball-bearing channels are the sensible standard for kitchens and wardrobes.
  • Tandem boxes and concealed channels give the smoothest, quietest drawers for heavy use.
  • Check the load rating and match the length to the drawer depth.

Locks and latches

  • Use a cam lock or wardrobe lock on drawers that hold valuables.
  • For main doors and cabin doors, choose a mortise lock with a quality cylinder over a cheap rim lock.
  • Prefer solid brass or stainless internals for long life.

Handles and profiles

  • Handles are the most visible part, so pick a finish that matches your taps and fixtures.
  • Profile (handleless) systems give a clean modern look but need good alignment and installation.
  • Test the grip. A handle you will pull a hundred times a week should feel comfortable.

Where to spend more and where to save

  • Spend more on: hinges, drawer channels and anything carrying weight.
  • Save on: decorative handles, which are easy to replace later.
  • Never skip: proper screws and fixings. Weak screws cause sagging doors.
